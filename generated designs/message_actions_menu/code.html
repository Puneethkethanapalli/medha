Explain the concept of quantum entanglement in simple terms, emphasizing its potential applications in secure communications.
10:42 AM

Imagine two coins that are magically linked. No matter how far apart they are—even across the galaxy—if you flip one and it lands on heads, the other will instantly land on heads too.

That "magical link" is quantum entanglement. In the quantum world, particles like photons can become entangled, meaning the state of one instantly determines the state of the other.

That's the beauty of it. You can't just intercept it without changing it.

In quantum mechanics, the act of observing a particle alters its state. If an eavesdropper tries to intercept the entangled photons used to create a cryptographic key (a process called Quantum Key Distribution, or QKD), the entanglement breaks, and the interference is immediately detected by the communicating parties.
